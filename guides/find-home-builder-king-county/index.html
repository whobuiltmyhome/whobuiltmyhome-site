How to find your home's builder

Start with the address, then check county records and the original building permit.

Updated November 13, 2026

Step 1

Find the parcel number

Search the address in King County Parcel Viewer ↗. Check the location and note the ten-digit parcel number. For a condo or subdivided lot, check that you have the right unit or lot.

You can also search this site by address, city, ZIP, or parcel number. It includes 16,025 homes matched to 9 builders.

Step 2

Check the property record

Open King County eReal Property ↗ and compare the address, year built, and sales history. On this site, each home's details include a link to its county property record.

Note the sale date and recording number. The sale date and year built refer to different events.

Step 3

Read the deed

Look up the recording number in the King County Recorder's search ↗. Check the property description and company names in the document.

Older documents may require a name or property-description search. If you cannot find a document online, check with the Recorder's Office or county Archives.

Step 4

Find the building permit

Check the property's jurisdiction in Parcel Viewer. Ask the city's permit office for the original construction permit. For unincorporated areas, contact King County Permits ↗. The city in a mailing address may differ from the permit jurisdiction.

Check the contractor's name, address or parcel, dates, and work described. Make sure the permit covers the original home, rather than an addition or remodel. Ask about archived files if older permits are not online.

Save the records

Keep the source links, recording numbers, permit numbers, and company names. If a home is missing from this site, check the county records directly.
